# Evidence model

## Three layers

1. **Source evidence** — one expert, one call, one assertion, exact paragraph anchors
2. **Claim cluster** — a normalized proposition that links supporting, contradicting, and qualifying source evidence
3. **Case map** — a compact routing index showing where knowledge and disagreement live

Never replace source evidence with synthesis. Synthesis can change as new calls arrive; provenance must remain traceable

## Atomicity test

An evidence record is atomic when another expert could agree with one part and disagree with another without ambiguity. Split records when they contain:

- separate metrics
- separate entities
- separate time periods
- separate causal claims
- separate product or customer scopes
- a factual observation plus a forecast

## Scope dimensions

Capture when material:

- time: historical, current, forecast, effective date, period
- geography
- product, service, component, platform, SKU, technology, or frame
- customer, segment, channel, account, or use case
- scenario: base, upcycle, downturn, stress, hypothetical
- process stage
- definition, numerator, denominator, currency, unit, and basis

## Source quality dimensions

- direct role and first-hand exposure
- recency
- specificity
- internal consistency
- independent corroboration
- caveats and knowledge boundaries
- estimate, calculation, recollection, opinion, forecast, or observed fact

Confidence is an assessment of the source evidence, not proof that the claim is true

## Provenance rule

Every material assertion must point to one or more exact `[P####]` transcript anchors. A source card or summary is not a substitute for the original anchor
