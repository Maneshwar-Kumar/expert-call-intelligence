# Expert call summary — house style guide

This guide is distilled from the user's Project Amsterdam expert-call compilation and is authoritative for all generated summaries

## Document structure

Each interview becomes one section

1. **Header** — `Interview — [Role/Title], [Company]`, or a numbered version in the consolidated master. Use the actual role and company. If the role is not explicit, infer the closest accurate description from context and do not overstate it
2. **Framing bullets** — two or three short bullets immediately below the header
   - **Stakeholder type:** which side of the value chain or market the expert represents
   - **Focus:** the analytical question the call was testing
   - **Note:** only when needed, for scope, authority, refusals, or segment limitations
3. **Thematic bullets** — the body of the summary
   - Begin with a bold, specific analytical lead, not a generic label
   - Follow with indented sub-bullets containing exact numbers, timeframes, named examples, attribution, and implications
   - Group related evidence under themes rather than following question order
   - End with the analytical implication where the transcript supports one

## What to strip out

- De-duplicate repeated speech-to-text clauses and garbled echoes
- Remove filler, false starts, acknowledgements, crosstalk, and thanks
- Remove interviewer scaffolding unless necessary to interpret the answer
- Keep all decision-relevant numbers, ranges, dates, currencies, units, denominators, and explicit hedges

## Tone and precision

- Use an analyst's synthesized voice, never Q&A and never the expert's first person
- Attribute claims: `the expert estimates`, `he recalled`, `she was direct that`, `the expert could not confirm`
- Paraphrase rather than quote at length
- Preserve ambiguity instead of guessing
- Do not invent missing numbers, names, or claims
- Separate what the expert knew directly from analogy, hearsay, calculation, or speculation

## Word mechanics

- Header uses Heading 2
- Framing and theme leads use top-level real Word bullets
- Theme leads are bold
- Framing labels through the colon are bold
- Detail uses second-level real Word bullets
- US Letter page size
- In the consolidated master, each interview starts on a new page and receives a stable first-seen number
