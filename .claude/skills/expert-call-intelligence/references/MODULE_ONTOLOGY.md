# Supplier, customer, competitor, and cross-cutting ontology

Use one evidence system with role-based module views. A company can be a supplier, customer, and competitor in different relationships or arenas

## Supplier module

Keep evidence on:

- products, components, services, processes, materials, technology, and size range
- qualification status by customer, part, platform, geography, and milestone
- qualification timeline, first part, serial production, reference history, and full maturity
- capacity, utilization, bottlenecks, committed expansion, announced capex, usable qualified capacity, and ramp timing
- share of wallet, sourcing allocation, primary/backup/single-source status
- pricing, ex-works and landed cost, margin, inflation pass-through, discounts, indexation, and price position
- contracts, LTA/MOU, annual agreements, take-or-pay, volume commitments, capacity reservation, and renewal
- quality, rejection, audit, delivery, OTD, lead time, warranty, and non-conformance
- geographic footprint, logistics, freight, tariffs, warehouse model, and regional proximity
- raw-material, sub-tier, equipment, labor, tooling, and special-process dependencies
- strategic relationships, co-investment, exclusivity, switching barriers, customer concentration, financial health, and downcycle risk
- supplier selection criteria and why business is won, lost, retained, or shifted

## Customer module

Distinguish **direct customer** from **end customer**

Keep evidence on:

- customer identity, segment, use case, geography, channel, and account importance
- demand drivers and demand inhibitors
- buying criteria, decision makers, procurement process, technical criteria, and switching thresholds
- initial discussion, soft reservation, paid reservation, firm order, PO, implementation, delivery, and renewal stages
- payments, deposits, forfeiture, cancellation, postponement, conversion, and backlog definitions
- current, historical, and forecast conversion rates by stage and segment
- price sensitivity, willingness to pay, urgency, service level, reliability, and unmet needs
- contract duration, milestones, volume commitments, renewal, churn, and share of spend
- installed base, replacement, repair, upgrade, aftermarket, and lifecycle demand
- customer concentration, account economics, pain points, and barriers to adoption

## Competitor module

Define the competitive arena precisely: product, service, customer, geography, technology, and time

Keep evidence on:

- direct competitor, adjacent player, substitute, entrant, incumbent, and partner-competitor roles
- technical capability, process coverage, qualification, accreditation, product breadth, and innovation
- market share and share of wallet, historical and forecast
- price, landed cost, margin, contract terms, and discounting
- capacity, utilization, lead time, quality, delivery, and geographic footprint
- customer relationships, channel access, installed base, strategic agreements, and switching barriers
- investments, new plants, partnerships, acquisitions, exits, and capacity announcements
- why each player wins or loses
- moat, vulnerability, execution gap, concentration, and financial risk
- likely behavior in an upcycle, downturn, customer shift, or regulatory change

## Cross-cutting module

Keep evidence on:

- market size, growth, cycle, scenarios, and demand-supply balance
- value chain and profit-pool movement
- regulation, policy, geopolitics, tariffs, and trade
- technology, substitution, standards, and product lifecycle
- definitions and denominators that affect interpretation
- industry structure, concentration, capacity, pricing, and bargaining power

## Topic labels

Prefer reusable lower-case kebab-case topics, for example:

`qualification-timeline`, `technical-capability`, `capacity`, `utilization`, `capex`, `share-of-wallet`, `market-share`, `pricing`, `landed-cost`, `contract-structure`, `quality`, `delivery`, `lead-time`, `supplier-selection`, `order-funnel`, `reservation-to-order-conversion`, `cancellation`, `backlog-definition`, `customer-demand`, `aftermarket-demand`, `replacement-cycle`, `competitive-position`, `geographic-footprint`, `raw-material-risk`, `switching-barrier`, `market-growth`, `downcycle-risk`
